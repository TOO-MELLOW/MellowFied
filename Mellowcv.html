1. What makes a CV truly “ATS‑friendly”?

Most people think ATS means “avoid tables and graphics”. That’s true, but it’s only the tip of the iceberg. Modern ATS (like Workday, Taleo, and Greenhouse) parse your CV into a structured profile. Here’s what they actually look for:

  • Standard section headings – “Work Experience”, “Education”, “Skills”, “Summary”. Creative labels like “My Journey” or “What I Do” confuse the parser.
  • Reverse‑chronological order – recruiters expect to see your most recent role first. Functional or hybrid CVs often flag as “inconsistent”.
  • Simple fonts and font sizes – Arial, Calibri, Lato, or Helvetica between 10–12pt. Serif fonts (like Times New Roman) are acceptable, but sans‑serif gives a cleaner digital read.
  • No columns (for most ATS) – while some newer systems handle two‑column layouts, the safest bet is a single‑column structure. MellowCV’s “ATS” templates use single‑column by default, while our “Modern” templates use a sidebar that still passes rigorous parsing tests.
  • Plain text bullets – use hyphens (`-`) or asterisks (`*`) instead of fancy icons or custom bullet graphics.
  • Save as PDF – but not a scanned image‑based PDF. MellowCV exports tagged PDFs with real selectable text.

2. The “Keyword Gap” – and how we fix it

An ATS doesn't just scan your CV , it scores it against the job description. If the job says “Project Management” and your CV says “Team Coordination”, the system might not see a match. That’s where MellowCV’s built‑in Keyword Scanner comes in.

  1. Paste the job description into MellowCV’s ATS panel.
  2. We extract all key nouns, verbs, and required qualifications.
  3. We highlight which keywords already exist in your CV, and which ones are missing.
  4. You get a real‑time “Match Score” not a generic percentage, but a checklist of exactly which skills and terms to add.

This isn’t “keyword stuffing”. It’s intelligent alignment. You still write in your own voice, but you never miss a critical requirement again.

3. CV structure: the anatomy of a winning CV

Based on data from recruitment agencies and HR analytics, the most successful CVs follow this structure:

  • Header – full name, job title, phone, email, location (city and country). No photo required unless you’re applying in regions where it’s customary (e.g., certain European or Asian markets).
  • Professional Summary – 3–4 sentences that summarise your value proposition, not just your job title. “Frontend developer with 3 years of experience” → “Frontend developer who increased conversion rates by 20% through performant, accessible interfaces.”
  • Core Skills / Competencies – a short, scannable list of 6–10 hard and soft skills. MellowCV presents these as clean chips or compact rows.
  • Work Experience – for each role: company name, location, dates, and 3–5 bullet points. Each bullet should follow the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and include numbers where possible. Example: “Reduced ticket resolution time by 30% by redesigning the support workflow.”
  • Education – degrees, institutions, graduation years, and relevant coursework or honours.
  • Additional sections – Certifications, Languages, Projects, Volunteer Work, or Interests (only if relevant). MellowCV allows you to reorder and toggle visibility for each section.
